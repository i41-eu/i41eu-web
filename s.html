Apps for your Stackchan robot

A pet for kids, a focus timer for your desk, and a dashboard of every sensor for the curious: apps for M5Stack's Stackchan robot. You pick the apps your robot gets and approve each one. Proofs of concept, vibe coded, not reviewed by humans yet.

The robot rendered in 3D from M5Stack's StackChan structure files (MIT), with real screens from it. Turn it round and see its sensors on s.i41.eu/s3d.

What you need: the robot from M5Stack, a computer with Chrome or Edge, a USB-C cable and 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.

m.i41.eustart here

The Stackchan manager online: sign in with GitHub or Google, pick the apps for your robot and set it up with one click over USB (Chrome or Edge): the official firmware, your Wi-Fi and a token of its own for every app you approve.

Then it keeps a live line to your robots: switch their app, restart them, add or remove apps, see who is paired. One sign-in works for every app.

Rather keep it at home? The same manager runs on a computer of your own, with no sign-in and no cloud: the home manager. All the ways to set up a robot: SETUP.

Apps

Each app has a host of its own under q41.eu, so no app can read another's data in your browser. On the robot, swipe up for the app switcher; on a phone, scan the robot's QR code to pair.

Pet

A Tamagotchi for kids, on the robot and on a phone: feed it, pet it, play with it, put it to bed, or hold an NFC card to it to feed it. It shows how it feels, and never suffers while the kid sleeps or is away.

Read more · open pet.q41.eu

Focus

A focus timer for your desk: 25 minutes of focus, then a break. Tap the robot to start or pause; it chimes, nods and dims its LEDs as time runs out. Tasks, a daily goal and the week's count on your phone.

Raw data

Every sensor and command: see through its camera, hear it, talk through it, move its head, set its face and LEDs, read IR remotes and NFC tags. With end to end encryption on, only paired browsers can read the robot: the server relays ciphertext.

Devnew

Your robot for your own programs and AI agents: show text on its screen, ask a question and get the button tapped, light the LEDs as a progress bar, play a chime, hear when someone pats its head. One curl per action, or MCP for Claude Code and other agents, with a key per robot. And a Go kit for writing new apps.

Read more · open dev.q41.eu

How it works

The robot runs Embody Mode, an app in its firmware that makes it a light client: it connects out to one app at a time over a WebSocket; nothing connects in to it. Each app gets a token for just that robot, from the manager or one you make yourself. The firmware is built reproducibly: anyone can rebuild a release and get the same bytes, and each release is signed.
